# George Orwell

**George Orwell** (pen name of **Eric Arthur Blair**; Motihari, India, 1903 –
London, 1950) is the author of ***1984*** (1949), the most famous **dystopian
novel** of the twentieth century and a lasting warning against **totalitarianism**.

## Life

Born in British India, Orwell was educated in England and then served in the
**Imperial Police in Burma**, an experience that turned him against **imperialism**.
Back in Europe he chose to live among the poor (*Down and Out in Paris and London*)
and became a committed **socialist**, deeply hostile to every form of tyranny. He
fought in the **Spanish Civil War**, where he saw at first hand how propaganda and
political lies could rewrite reality — an experience that shaped his two greatest
books, the satire ***Animal Farm*** (1945) and ***1984*** (1949). He died of
tuberculosis in 1950, shortly after finishing *1984*.

## What is dystopian fiction?

A **dystopia** is the opposite of a **utopia**: instead of an ideal, perfect
society, it imagines a **nightmarish future** in which things have gone terribly
wrong. Dystopian fiction usually portrays a **totalitarian state** that controls
every aspect of life — often through technology, surveillance, propaganda and the
manipulation of language and history — and follows an individual who tries, and
generally fails, to rebel against it. It works as a **warning**: by pushing the
tendencies of the present to their extreme, it asks the reader to recognise and
resist them. *1984* is the model of the genre.

### *1984* (1949)

The novel is set in a future **London**, capital of **Airstrip One**, a province
of the super-state **Oceania**. The world is divided among three super-powers —
Oceania, **Eurasia** and **Eastasia** — locked in a **perpetual, never-ending
war** whose only purpose is to keep the population in fear and consume resources.
Oceania is ruled by **the Party**, headed by the all-seeing figure of **Big
Brother**, whose face stares from every poster above the slogan **"Big Brother is
watching you"**.

**Main characters:**

- **Winston Smith** — the protagonist, a middle-aged Party member who works at the
  **Ministry of Truth**, secretly hates the regime and longs for freedom and truth.
- **Julia** — a younger woman who rebels in a private, instinctive way; she and
  Winston become lovers.
- **O'Brien** — a powerful member of the **Inner Party**; Winston believes he is a
  fellow rebel, but O'Brien is the agent who entraps, tortures and "re-educates" him.
- **Big Brother** — the symbolic leader of the Party, who may not exist as a real
  person at all but only as an **image of absolute power**.
- **Emmanuel Goldstein** — the (possibly invented) Enemy of the People, object of
  the daily **Two Minutes Hate**, and supposed author of the forbidden book that
  explains how the Party's society really works.

???+ abstract "Summary of the novel"

    **Winston Smith** lives in a grey, decaying London under the total
    surveillance of **telescreens**. He works at the **Ministry of Truth**
    (Minitrue), where his job is to **rewrite history** — altering newspapers and
    records so that the past always matches the Party's current version of the
    truth. Secretly hating the regime, he begins a forbidden **diary** and starts
    a love affair with **Julia**: their relationship is itself a **political act**
    of rebellion, because the Party forbids private loyalties and desire.

    They are drawn in by **O'Brien**, whom Winston trusts as a secret member of
    the resistance (the "Brotherhood") and who gives him **Goldstein's book**.
    But O'Brien is a Party agent: the lovers are arrested and taken to the
    **Ministry of Love** (Miniluv), a place of interrogation and torture. There
    O'Brien systematically breaks Winston down to make him **love Big Brother**.
    The final stage is **Room 101**, where each prisoner faces their own worst
    fear — for Winston, rats. Terrified, he betrays Julia ("Do it to Julia!"),
    and with that betrayal his inner resistance collapses. Released, emptied and
    defeated, Winston finally **loves Big Brother**.

**The Party's instruments of control:**

- **The Ministry of Truth (Minitrue)** uses the **media** to control people's
  ideas, constantly falsifying news and history so reality itself becomes whatever
  the Party says it is.
- **Newspeak** — the official language, deliberately **reduced and impoverished**
  so that "thoughtcrime" becomes literally **unthinkable**: if there are no words
  for rebellion, there can be no rebellious thoughts.
- **Doublethink** — the ability to hold **two contradictory beliefs** at once and
  accept both; together with **blackwhite** (calling black white when the Party
  demands it) and **crimestop** (the instinct to stop short of any dangerous
  thought).
- **The three slogans** of the Party — **"WAR IS PEACE — FREEDOM IS SLAVERY —
  IGNORANCE IS STRENGTH"** — sum up its use of contradiction as a tool of power.

**Themes:**

- **Totalitarianism** and the destruction of the individual.
- The **manipulation of truth, history and language** as a means of power.
- **Mass surveillance** ("Big Brother is watching you") and the loss of privacy.
- The **failure of rebellion** and the crushing of love and human feeling.

!!! tip "Connections"
    *1984* is the model of **dystopian fiction**; compare it with Orwell's own
    *Animal Farm* and with the broader twentieth-century reflection on
    totalitarianism. The theme of language as control (Newspeak) and of a world
    that has lost its bearings echoes the **crisis of the modern subject** explored
    in Italian literature by [Pirandello](../../letteratura/03-decadentismo/04-narrativa-della-crisi/02-luigi-pirandello.md).
